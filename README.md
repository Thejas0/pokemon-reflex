# Pokémon Open World

A browser game for personal use with real-time battles. It runs on a laptop or a phone, needs no build step, and has no dependencies.

## Play

- **Laptop:** open `index.html` in a browser.
- **Phone, on the same Wi-Fi as your laptop:**
  ```sh
  python3 -m http.server 8000
  ```
  Then open `http://<your-laptop-ip>:8000` on the phone (find the IP with `ipconfig getifaddr en0`). Landscape works best, and "Add to Home Screen" gives full screen.

Progress saves automatically to the browser's localStorage, separately on each device.

## Controls

| | Laptop | Mobile |
|---|---|---|
| Move | WASD / arrows | drag on the left half of the screen |
| Moves 1–4 | `1 2 3 4` or `J K L ;` | move buttons |
| Dodge roll / Ultimate (battle) | `Tab` / `U` | Dodge / Ultimate buttons |
| Pixel-art / smooth battle view | `P` (in battle) | |
| Poké Ball / change ball type / Switch / Run | `Q` / `B` / `E` / `R` | buttons |
| Menu (party, box, save) | `Esc` | Menu button |
| Bicycle on/off | `C` | 🚲 button |
| Town Map | `M` (or `N`) | 🗺️ button |
| Classic top-down / optional 2.5D overworld | `V` | |

## Rules

- **World:** walking into a visible wild Pokémon starts a battle, and so can tall grass. Wild Pokémon get stronger the farther you go from the starting town. Walk into a Poké Center to heal at the nurse's counter, manage storage at the PC, and check your Pokédex (all 251 Pokémon of Gen 1 and Gen 2, with Kanto/Johto filters). Poké Balls are never free: buy Poké, Great, Ultra or Master Balls inside the Poké Mart. Legendary Pokémon glow gold in the wild, are very rare, always appear at Lv 95-100 wherever you find them, have 60% higher HP, Attack, Defense and Speed than their level suggests, fight with near-perfect AI (`LEGENDARY_*` in `js/data.js`) and are very hard to catch; only a Master Ball is sure to work. The eight gyms are marked on the minimap; each gym town is farther from home (and tougher) than the last.
- **Bicycle:** buy it once at any Poké Mart (₽5,000) and press `C` / tap 🚲 to ride at about 1.8× walking speed. You hop off to swim.
- **Day and night:** the overworld runs a day/night cycle (12 minutes per day, `DAY_LENGTH` in `js/data.js`) with sunrise/sunset tints, lit doorways and street lamps, fireflies at night, and butterflies and falling leaves by day.
- **Lingering attacks:** Sludge Bomb and Acid leave a poison pool, and Fire Blast leaves burning ground, for 2–2.5 s. A Pokémon standing in it loses about 2% of its max HP every half second (Poison / Fire types are immune to their own kind). Move buttons mark these with ☠ / 🔥.
- **Sound & music:** chiptune music for the overworld (day and night), towns, wild battles, gyms and the League, plus sound effects for every move type, hits, catches, level-ups and menus. All synthesized in the browser (no audio files). Adjust or mute it in Menu (`Esc`) → Options. Sound starts after your first key press or tap.
- **Swimming:** water-type Pokémon learn **Swim** at Lv 10 (older ones can learn it from Menu → Moves). With a Swim (or Surf) user in your party you can cross water; ocean Pokémon like Lapras (only found at sea), Tentacool and Staryu appear while you swim.
- **PP:** every move has limited uses per battle (Recover and other heals 5, the strongest attacks 12–15, medium ones 23, weak ones 32, stat moves 12). PP refills each battle. When every move is out of PP the Pokémon uses Struggle, which hurts the user too. In battle, `B` (or ⇅) switches ball type.
- **Battles:** both Pokémon move freely around an arena. Moves are melee, dash, projectile, area, or telegraphed strikes. Projectiles and strikes can be dodged.
- **Arena-fighter moves:** tap a move to use it, or **hold** it to charge (up to +60% power and a bigger hitbox, at the cost of a longer cooldown). **Tab** dodge-rolls (you can't be hit mid-roll; no direction = sidestep). Rolling is tiring: each dodge uses 40% of the tiredness bar (0.6s at full HP), and you can only dodge when at least that much stamina is left (right after attacking you must wait for it to recover; "Too tired to dodge!" shows otherwise). The same rule applies to AI trainers, even against your ultimate, so nobody can spam it (`DODGE_TIREDNESS` in `js/data.js`). Hits landed in quick succession build a **combo** (up to +40% damage). Dealing and taking damage fills the **special meter**; when it's full press **U** for your Pokémon's type **Ultimate** (e.g. Charizard's Inferno Overdrive), a huge strike with a cinematic cut-in. Every type's ultimate looks different: its own cut-in backdrop (flames, crashing waves, a storm, a black hole, spinning gears...), its own incoming attack (a meteor, a giant icicle, a tornado, a psychic eye, fists closing in, a drill) and its own impact (a fire pillar, a geyser, a blooming flower, ice spikes, a POW burst, an implosion). Gym leaders, tournament trainers and legendaries use all of this too: they dodge-roll your attacks, punish you while you're tired, charge big moves and time their ultimates. They also vary their attacks: moves are picked by a weighted draw among the good options, and repeating the same move (or the same contact/ranged style) is discouraged, so even top trainers don't spam one move.
- **Overworld view:** the map is the classic flat top-down 2D view. Press `V` if you ever want to try the optional 2.5D view (tilted ground, upright trees, buildings and characters); press it again to go back. The choice is remembered.
- **Stadium battles (Pokémon Arena 2D style):** gym and tournament battles are played in a stadium with packed stands above and below the field (the crowd jumps and waves signs on big hits), a banner bar showing the match-up ("POKÉMON LEAGUE · BLASTOISE VS JOLTEON"), and a couple of mossy boulders that block movement and projectiles. The League field is scuffed dirt with worn chalk lines; the Water gym is deep halftone water with floating Poké Ball rafts and buoys. Heavy hits leave craters, cracks and scorch marks that stay for the whole battle. Battles are drawn in a chunky pixel-art style (press `P` for the smooth look); names and numbers stay sharp. Hydro Pump is a twisting torrent, electric hits craze the floor like cracked glass, and damage numbers are big outlined arcade digits.
- **Wild starters:** the starters can be found in the wild too, but rarely (about 1 in 100 encounters in their area, from Lv 5): Bulbasaur and Chikorita in grass and forests, Charmander and Cyndaquil in rocky mountains and grass, Squirtle and Totodile in water. Each only in its own region. Crobat, Steelix, Scizor, Kingdra, Porygon2 and Blissey (Johto evolutions of Kanto Pokémon) are also rare finds in Johto's wild from Lv 30-35, so every one of the 251 Pokémon can be found somewhere.
- **Movepools:** 120 moves in all. Besides the originals there are classics like Slam, Slash, Take Down, Double-Edge, the elemental punches, Mach Punch, Brick Break, Flame Wheel, Waterfall, Crabhammer, Blizzard, Drill Peck, Bonemerang, Future Sight, Dragon Claw and Dragon Breath, and every evolution line learns more of them by level (Pikachu: Slam 18, Iron Tail 26, Agility 34, Thunder Punch 38, Double-Edge 45). Pokémon that are already past a level can still pick the move up from the Moves screen ("Ready to learn") in the party menu or the Poké Center PC. Extra moves live in `js/learnsets.js`.
- **Fire Blast** is shot from the user as the classic five-armed "大" flame: it only hits if it touches the target (dodge it or step aside), then leaves the ground burning.
- **Signature moves:** every damaging move plays its own effect when it lands: Surf and Hydro Pump whip water crescents around the target, Fire Fang / Bite / Crunch snap jaws shut, Thunderbolt cages it in lightning, Solar Beam and Sacred Fire raise pillars of light and fire, Stone Edge bursts rock spikes out of the floor, Dynamic Punch lands a giant fist, Swift and Ancient Power circle stars and rocks around it, and so on for all 79 attacks (`js/movefx.js`).
- **Arenas:** every battlefield is animated: swaying grass and bushes, lake shine and reeds by water, lava pools and torches in fire gyms, circuit lines in electric gyms, candles and fog in ghost gyms. Gym and League battles have a cheering crowd in the stands that roars on big hits, ultimates and knockouts. Wild battles follow the time of day: warm at dusk, dark at night with spotlights on both Pokémon.
- **Close range vs. ranged:** melee moves leap in if the target is just out of reach, contact moves (melee and dash) cost 35% less cooldown and briefly stagger the target, dashing Pokémon pass through projectiles, and projectiles lose up to 30% power over long distances. Pokémon whose damaging moves are mostly contact moves are 15% faster. Tune all of this in `js/data.js`.
- **Tiredness:** after every move a Pokémon must wait
  `1.5s × (1 + 1.5 × (maxHP − HP) / maxHP)`.
  That is 1.5s at full HP and 3.75s near 0 HP. Tune it in `js/data.js` (`MOVE_COOLDOWN_BASE`, `TIREDNESS_FACTOR`).
- **Gyms:** walk inside each gym. A gym guide explains the challenge, gym trainers battle you when you walk up to them, and each gym has its own puzzle: Misty's drain valves, Lt. Surge's hidden trash-can switches, Erika's hedge maze, Koga's invisible walls, Sabrina's warp pads, Blaine's quiz statues (a wrong answer summons a trainer) and Giovanni's switch order. The leader only battles once their trainers are beaten and the puzzle is solved; then pick up to 3 Pokémon in order. After the leader battle your whole team is restored to full health. Progress inside each gym is saved, and dialogue advances with Enter / Space or a tap.
- **Moves:** reorder a Pokémon's moves (which one is on keys 1–4) in its Moves screen, e.g. from the PC in any Poké Center.
- **Pokémon League:** with all 8 badges, enter the tournament at Indigo Plateau (gold ★ on the minimap): 32 trainers, single elimination, 5 matches to become Champion. Matches are full 6-on-6; your opponent's team stays hidden until the battle, and opponents get higher levels and sharper AI every round. In Kanto, opponents never go above Lv 78, and your rival Blue always fields his team at Lv 80 (`levelCap` and `rival.level` in `js/johto.js`). Your team is fully healed after every match. Each round is on a different stage: Grass Field (tall grass speeds up Grass/Bug types, slows others), Lakeside Pools (Water types faster, others wade), Rocky Ground (boulders block movement and projectiles), Forest Clearing (grass and rocks) and the Champion Stadium. Prize money each round; a Master Ball for the Champion. If you lose you can start again from the Round of 32 with a new bracket; once you win you are Champion for good. While `TOURNAMENT_DEBUG` is `true` in `js/data.js` you can also enter without badges, re-enter after winning, and retry a single lost match ("Continue").
- **Johto (Gen 2):** win the Kanto League and a portal opens in every Poké Center, leading to New Bark Town in Johto. Your Kanto Pokémon stay behind (they're kept safe in Kanto), you pick Chikorita, Cyndaquil or Totodile, and only Gen 2 Pokémon (#152–251, including the new Dark and Steel types) live in the wild. Johto has 16 walk-in gyms, each with its own route and puzzle, including Morty's and Karen's pitch-dark gyms and Pryce's sliding ice floor. With all 16 badges you can enter the Silver Conference: a 64-trainer, 6-round tournament where your Kanto Pokémon may fight alongside your Johto team. Use the portal at any Poké Center to travel between the regions; each region keeps its own team, box and tournament, while money, items and the Pokédex are shared.
- **Growth:** Pokémon gain XP from every win or catch, learn moves as they level, and evolve. If a Pokémon already knows 4 moves, you choose one to forget or skip the new move. You can postpone an evolution with "Not now".

## Code

`js/data.js` holds tuning, types, moves, species and gyms. `pokemon.js` handles stats, XP, learning and evolution. `world.js` is the map and wild spawns, `battle.js` the real-time engine and AI, `interior.js` the walk-in Poké Center and Mart, `tournament.js` the League bracket, `gyms.js` the walk-in gyms (layouts, story, puzzles), `johto.js` / `johto-gyms.js` the Gen 2 species, moves, region settings and gyms, `vfx.js` the battle arenas and move effects (cosmetic only), `ults.js` the per-type ultimate visuals, `stadium.js` the stadium stands and pixel-art setting, `movefx.js` the per-move hit effects, `learnsets.js` the extra moves and level-up moves, `audio.js` the music and sound effects, `ui.js` the menus and HUD, and `main.js` the game loop and saving.

Pokémon sprites (from the PokeAPI sprite collection) are bundled in `sprites/<dex>.png`, so they work offline. If one is missing, the game fetches it from the jsDelivr CDN; if that fails too, a colored placeholder is drawn.
